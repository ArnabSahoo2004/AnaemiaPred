A B S T R A C T
In developing countries like Tanzania, despite the national intervention, the proportion of anaemic children aged 
6–59 months is seen to be high, with a prevalence of 59 %. Traditional methods, such as examining paleness in 
the eyes and tongue, are commonly used, but are subjective and often lead to delayed or missed diagnoses. While 
existing Machine learning models have attempted to predict anaemia in children and offer improved accuracy, 
many rely on single-model strategies and a default threshold of 0.5, which tends to favour sensitivity over 
specificity, leading to a high number of false positives. The study used the supervised machine learning approach 
within the CRISP-DM framework. A stacked hybrid approach was used, integrating Random Forest (RF) and 
Artificial Neural Network (ANN) as base models, using XGBoost as a meta-learner. The model’s performance was 
evaluated using metrics such as accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, precision, and Area Under the Curve (AUC) with 
95 % confidence intervals (CIs) across thresholds of 0.35, 0.4, 0.45, and 0.5, optimized using Youden’s J index. 
The hybrid model achieved balanced performance, especially at a 0.4 threshold with a sensitivity of 0.861 and a 
specificity of 0.880. Compared to standalone models, the hybrid approach outperformed in reducing false pos­
itives and false negatives, offering greater reliability and clinical safety. This study concludes that the stacking 
ensemble approach, along with threshold optimization, provides an effective solution for early detection of 
anaemia in children. Integration of hybrid Machine Learning models into Tanzania’s health screening programs 
could improve child health outcomes.
1. Introduction
Anaemia refers to a condition characterized by a decrease in the 
number or quality of red blood cells, and remains a serious global health 
concern, particularly affecting young children, menstruating adolescent 
girls, and pregnant and postpartum women (Gardner et al., 2023). Ac­
cording to the World Health Organization (WHO), the proportion of 
anaemia varies across different groups worldwide, with 40 % of children 
aged 6–59 months, 37 % of pregnant women, and 30 % of women aged 
15–49 years affected (Gardner et al., 2023). In developing countries like 
Tanzania, despite the national intervention, the situation is still critical, 
with 59 % of children in this group suffering from anaemia, according to 
the Tanzania Demographic and Health Survey (Ministry of Health 
(MoH)[Tanzania Mainland] et al., 2022).
Current clinical screening methods are often subjective and can lead 
to delayed or missed diagnoses, hindering timely intervention to erad­
icate the problem. To tackle this problem, there is an urgent need to 
improve the prediction methods that use modern data analytics and 
machine learning techniques to enhance the prediction and timeliness of 
anaemia diagnosis in children aged 6–59 months.
Existing machine learning studies have limited performance metrics, 
such that the sensitivity and accuracy show higher performance than 
specificity, especially in an imbalanced dataset like the health dataset, 
which indicates many false positives, as many non-diseased (non- 
anaemic) children might be incorrectly classified as diseased (anaemic), 
leading to unnecessary treatment or misallocation of medical resources. 
Lower specificity indicates the possibility of missed cases. This is 
because most of these studies used the traditional default threshold of 
0.5, which optimizes only one metric. Additionally, most of these studies 
relied on a single model strategy.

Hybrid machine learning model using a stacking approach that com­
bines Random Forest (RF), a tree-based model and Artificial Neural 
Networks (ANN), a deep learning model, (RF+ANN) with an XGBoost 
model, a meta-learner integrating with the threshold optimization using 
Youden’s index to ensure a balanced sensitivity and specificity for the

2. Background review
Anaemia is a condition characterized by a lower-than-normal 
amount of red blood cells or a haemoglobin concentration that is 
<110 g/L at sea level, and hence leads to paleness, fatigue, dizziness, 
and difficulty breathing (Gardner et al., 2023). Bate and Rasmussen 
(2009) mentioned that Iron deficiency is the primary cause of anaemia 
in children due to insufficient iron intake, deficiencies in vitamins like 
vitamin A and vitamin B12, infections such as malaria, tuberculosis, 
HIV, and parasitic infections that impair nutrient absorption and 
metabolism, and inherited red blood cell disorders, including gene 
variants, and sickle cell that change the haemoglobin structure. If left 
untreated, anaemia directly affects the child’s immune system, physical 
and cognitive development, and academic performance.
2.1. Factors associated with the cause of anaemia in children aged 6–59 
months
Studies conducted by Kejo et al. (2018); Msaki et al. (2022); Said 
et al. (2021) reported that the factors such as the child’s age, low birth 
weight, nonconsumption of iron-rich foods like meat, vegetables, and 
fruits, being a mother with no schooling, being a male child, being a 
child within the 6–59 months age, home births, maternal anaemia, 
child’s stunting, and fever. These factors are categorized into Social, 
demographic, and health-related factors.
2.2. Feature selection techniques
Feature selection techniques are the methods applied to select or 
identify the subset of the most appropriate features from the dataset 
while leaving out the redundant or irrelevant ones to improve the 
model’s interpretability, computational efficiency, and predictive per­
formance (Laakel Hemdanou et al., 2024). There are three types of 
feature selection techniques: Filter methods, such as Chi-square tests, 
correlation, and mutual information; Wrapper methods, including 
Recursive Feature Elimination (RFE); and Embedded methods, including 
LASSO and Random Forest.
Most studies have used the filter method to select the best or sig­
nificant features based on statistical relevance (Tesfaye et al., 2024; 
Uwizera et al., 2022; Zahirzada et al., 2023). Other previous studies 
have shown the superiority of the Embedded feature selection technique 
using random forest, as it selects the relevant features and is a capable 
solution to high-dimensional datasets that contain interactions among 
features of different types (ElDahshan et al., 2023; Saihood, 2022). In 
addition, using Random Forest as the feature selection technique, 
evaluates each feature’s importance during the model training process, 
features with higher importance scores are selected for model building, 
and those with lower threshold scores are ignored (Pudjihartono et al., 
2022).
2.3. Machine learning models in the prediction of anaemia
Recent studies have discovered the potential of Machine learning 
(ML) models that offer more objective and timely diagnoses compared to 
traditional methods, since the proper intervention and diagnosis of 
anaemia in children 6–59 months can reduce the risk of anaemia, and 
enhance the detection of anaemia based on multiple risk factors 
(Uwizera et al., 2022). Several models, such as Random Forest (RF), 
XGBoost classifier, Support Vector Machine (SVM), Logistic Regression 
(LR), Naïve Bayes (NB), K-Nearest Neighbor (K-NN), Multi-Layer Per­
ceptron (MLP), decision tree, and penalized regression models such as 
ridge, LASSO, and elastic net have been used to predict anaemia in 
children aged 6–59 months. A study by Uwizera et al. (2022) found that 
the XGBoost model was the best with an Accuracy of 85.21 %, Sensitivity 
of 98.71 %, Specificity of 71.70 %, F1-score of 86.97 %, and AUC of 98 
%. Another study reported that RF outperformed the other models with 
an Accuracy of 86.4 %, AUC of 88.2 %, Precision of 85.0 %, and recall of 
77.3 % (Zahirzada et al., 2023). (Tesfaye et al. (2024) showed that the 
LR model was the best model with an Accuracy of 66 %, Sensitivity of 82 
%, Specificity of 42 %, and AUC of 69 %. Another study reported that 
LASSO had the highest performance with a Kappa of 28.56 %, an Ac­
curacy of 64.29 %, a Sensitivity of 62.24 %, a Specificity of 66.32 %, a 
Positive predictive value of 64.71 %, a Negative predictive value of 
63.89 %, and an F1 value of 63.45 %.
2.4. Evaluation metrics
A study conducted by Uwizera et al. (2022) considered accuracy, 
sensitivity, specificity, F1 score, and Receiver Operating Characteristics- 
area under the curve (ROC-AUC) as evaluation metrics. A study by 
Tesfaye et al. (2024) considered performance metrics such as Accuracy, 
Sensitivity, Specificity, and ROC-AUC. Accuracy, AUC, Precision, and 
Recall were the measures for assessing the model’s performance 
(Zahirzada et al., 2023). Another study conducted to predict anaemia in 
children aged 6–59 months considered performance metrics such as 
Kappa, Accuracy, Sensitivity, Specificity, Positive predictive value, 
Negative predictive value, and F1 value for evaluation of the models 
(Meitei et al., 2022), while another study considered accuracy, sensi­
tivity, and specificity as the evaluation metrics (Saihood, 2022).
This study used evaluation metrics such as accuracy, precision, 
sensitivity, specificity, and AUC. The 95 % confidence intervals (CIs) 
were computed using a non-parametric bootstrap procedure with 1000 
resamples. Each resample was drawn with replacement from the test set, 
and the metric distribution across distribution resamples was used to 
calculate the percentile-based CI bounds. This approach avoids the 
assumption of normality and is widely used in classification perfor­
mance evaluation (Skretteberg et al., 2024).
2.5. Threshold optimization
Threshold optimization in machine learning classification refers to 
the cut point or boundary used to assign a specific predicted class to each 
entity, and it is an important parameter when building and evaluating 
classification models, significantly affecting the model’s performance 
(Guide, 2025). As is known, the traditional default threshold is 0.5 and is 
widely used in machine learning classification tasks, and the goal is to 
optimize a single performance metric, such as accuracy. However, some 
situations require more attention and are essential to maximize two or 
more performance metrics simultaneously to ensure balanced and 
robust classifier performance (Foody, 2023).
Lowering the threshold helps to balance the performance metrics. A 
study conducted by Foody (2023) reported that the optimal threshold of 
0.3 provided a better balance among the three evaluation metrics 
compared to the default threshold of 0.5, the most significant metric, 
sensitivity, improved from 72.65 % to 84.65 %, and using the health 
dataset to identify the malignant tumors reported that the optimal 
threshold of 0.316 achieved the balance between sensitivity and speci­
ficity, indicating that the model identified a greater number of positive 
cases and reduced the number of false negatives. Another study con­
ducted by Deo et al. (2024) reported that after applying the threshold

2.6. Related works
Some more recent work uses advanced techniques such as spatial 
attention in images or aggregate model ranking (HBADR). A study 
conducted by Asare et al. (2023) considered features such as Hb values, 
age, sex, and remark (anaemic or non-anaemic) based on the Hb value 
captured with the corresponding images from the conjunctiva of the 
eyes, palpable palm, and fingernails and uploaded to the cloud storage 
(database) as the significant factors for anaemia detection in children 
using machine learning models such as Naïve Bayes, K-NN, SVM, Con­
volutional neural network (CNN), and decision tree. The CNN achieved 
a higher accuracy of 99.12 % compared to other models, indicating the 
power of CNN and the performance of the models, which explains that 
the non-invasive approach is an active instrument for anaemia 
detection.
Another study used the CP-AnaemiC dataset, the largest publicly 
available conjunctiva pallor-based dataset with annotated images of 
balanced anaemic and non-anaemic classes. Two representative tasks in 
anaemia diagnosis in children were performed, including Hb levels 
prediction and anaemia label classification. The study developed a deep 
neural network-based joint classification and regression learning 
framework, which simultaneously classifies anaemia and estimates Hb 
levels (g/dL) using the conjunctiva pallor images. The experimental 
results showed the effectiveness of the joint deep neural network in both 
the tasks of anaemia classification and Hb levels (g/dL) estimation 
(Appiahene et al., 2023).
Another study used the conjunctiva images to detect anaemia in 
children using machine learning models such as KNN, SVM, CNN, Naive 
Bayes, and Decision Tree. The CNN achieved a higher accuracy of 98.45 
% indicating the importance of detecting anaemia in children using non- 
invasive techniques (Asare et al., 2024).
A study by Saputra et al. (2025) used an ensemble model that in­
tegrates Random Forest, XGBoost, and sea horse optimization tech­
niques to determine the optimal number and depth of trees, hence 
improving accuracy and interpretability. The study also included an 
explainable artificial intelligence through SHAP analysis for trans­
parency and trust in clinical decision-making. The study concluded that 
the Smart-Forest outperformed traditional models with an accuracy of 
98.82 %, precision of 98.09 %, recall of 99.39 %, and F1-score of 98.72, 
indicating the reliability of the predictive power and the model’s 
balanced nature.
Additionally, the study reviewed several related works to identify the 
Social, demographic, and health-related factors associated with the 
cause of anaemia in children aged 6–59 months, and the machine 
learning models used together with the evaluation metrics.
Uwizera et al. (2022) identified several key social, demographic, and 
health-related factors, including a child’s age, malaria history, duration 
of breastfeeding, Residence, and Type of toilet facilities, as significant 
predictors of anaemia in children. The five machine learning models, 
such as RF, XGBoost, SVM, LR, and NB, were developed and evaluated. 
The XGBoost outperformed the other models with an Accuracy of 85.21 
%, Sensitivity of 98.71 %, Specificity of 71.70 %, F1-score of 86.97 %, 
and AUC of 98 %. Thus, XGBoost was found to be reliable for detecting 
anaemic cases; however, it has a relatively low chance of identifying 
actual negative cases.
A study by Tesfaye et al. (2024) found that maternal and household 
factors such as maternal anaemia and weight, place of residence, and 
number of children under five as the most significant features for 
childhood anaemia. The models used included LR, RF, KNN, and DT. The 
LR model was the best model with an Accuracy of 66 %, Sensitivity of 82 
%, Specificity of 42 %, and AUC of 69 %. This indicates that most of the 
anaemic children were correctly detected, while many non-anaemic 
children were incorrectly classified as anaemic. The model also had a 
fair chance (69 %) of correctly distinguishing between anaemic and 
non-anaemic children.
The study conducted in Afghanistan considered the child, maternal, 
and household-related factors such as maternal anaemia and weight, 
residence, and wealth status, as the most significant features for anaemia 
prediction in children aged 6–59 months. The ML models used were K- 
NN, MLP, NB, RF, and SVM. The RF outperformed the other models with 
an Accuracy of 86.4 %, an AUC of 88.2 %, a Precision of 85.0 %, and a 
recall of 77.3 %, indicating that the model was generally reliable in 
predicting anaemia status, correctly distinguished between an anaemic 
and a non-anaemic child, and the model correctly identified 77.3 % of 
the actual anaemic cases (Zahirzada et al., 2023).
In addition to model selection, several studies have emphasized the 
importance of hyperparameter tuning that yields the best performance 
for a machine learning model. Trouvain et al. (2020) urged that the 
random search gets more samples from the important parameters, while 
the grid search undersamples the space by repeating the sampling pro­
cess for important parameters. A study conducted by Ramzan et al. 
(2024) reported using the GridSearchCV to enhance the performance of 
the models. Some studies that apply SVMs or Random Forests to clas­
sification tasks frequently report using grid search (Chicco & Jurman, 
2020) and others, cross-validation-based tuning of hyperparameters 
(Uwizera et al., 2022). Similarly, the Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) 
often require optimization of learning rate, number of hidden units, and 
dropout rates (A Ilemobayo et al., 2024). In this study, however, 
hyperparameter tuning was performed using a manual search and 
literature-guided approach, rather than automated search techniques 
such as grid search or randomized search, where values for each model 
were selected based on evidence from prior studies (Goodfellow, 2015; 
Jin et al., 2020).
A study conducted by Meitei et al. (2022) considered the social, 
demographic, and health-related features such as the child’s age and sex, 
number of living children, sanitation, and disposal of the youngest 
child’s stool, as the most predictors of anaemia in children. The study 
used penalized regression models such as ridge, LASSO, and elastic net. 
LASSO achieved the highest performance with a Kappa of 28.56 %, an 
Accuracy of 64.29 %, a Sensitivity of 62.24 %, a Specificity of 66.32 %, a 
positive predictive value of 64.71 %, a negative predictive value of 
63.89 %, and an F1 value of 63.45 %. Indicating that the model had a 
balanced but moderate ability to detect true anaemic and non-anaemic 
cases, and the Kappa value indicated fair agreement beyond chance, 
showing that while the model performs better than random guessing, 
there is still considerable room for improvement.
While these studies have mainly relied on the image-based CNN 
models (Appiahene et al., 2023; Asare et al., 2023, 2024), used indi­
vidual models such as Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost 
(Meitei et al., 2022; Tesfaye et al., 2024; Uwizera et al., 2022; Zahirzada 
et al., 2023). Additionally, Saputra et al. (2025) used advanced 
ensemble modelling through the Smart-Forest, combining RF and 
XGBoost with optimization and SHAP analysis for interpretability, but 
this design was restricted to the tree-based learners. On the other hand, 
this study advances the literature by combining the strengths of the two 
base learners, Random Forest (RF) and Artificial Neural Network (ANN), 
with XGBoost as a meta learner. This combination allows the comple­
ments of tree-based methods (robustness to feature interactions and 
non-linear relationships) and neural networks (capacity to capture 
complex patterns). This framework has not been reported previously in 
the area of predicting anaemia in children, specifically in Tanzania. 
Additionally, unlike the previous studies that mainly rely on the stan­
dard decision threshold of 0.5, this study incorporated the threshold 
optimization technique based on Youden’s J index that optimizes the 
classification threshold to ensure a balanced trade-off between sensi­
tivity and specificity, which is important in unbalanced datasets such as

2.7. Study gap
Despite these advancements in the related works, the gaps remain. 
Many existing machine learning models for the prediction of anaemia in 
children aged 6–59 months are faced with the problem of imbalance in 
performance metrics specifically in health dataset that might have the 
positive (anaemic) cases as the majority cases and negative (non- 
anaemic) cases as the minority cases, where the sensitivity shows the 
higher performance and specificity lower performance, representing 
many false positives as many non-anaemic children might be incorrectly 
classified as anaemic. This is because most of these studies used the 
traditional default threshold of 0.5, which favors only one metric on the 
cost of another metric (higher sensitivity and lower specificity). Addi­
tionally, most of these studies relied on a single machine learning model 
for anaemia prediction.
This study addressed these study gaps by integrating the strengths of 
more than one machine learning models, a Hybrid machine learning 
model using a stacking ensemble approach that combined Random 
Forest (RF), and Artificial Neural Network (ANN) (RF+ANN) with a 
meta-learner XGBoost model, as a result this approach increases the 
model’s predictability power and generalizability with a threshold 
optimization approach that offered a balanced sensitivity and specificity 
that reduces the number of false positives (not anaemic children being 
classified as anaemic) and false negatives (anaemic children being 
classified as not anaemic).
3. Methods and tools
3.1. Study area
The study focused on urban and rural areas considering all usual 
residents in the households, including all women of reproductive age 
(15–49 years), men aged 15–49, and all children aged 0–59 months in 
the households across all regions of the Tanzanian Mainland and Zan­
zibar as covered by the Tanzania Demographic and Health Survey 
(2022). This survey was designed to provide representative estimates for 
the entire country and is conducted every five years.
3.2. Research plan
The Cross-Industry Standard Process for Data Mining (CRISP-DM) 
framework guides this study (Schr¨oer et al., 2021). The framework 
consists of six phases: Business understanding, data understanding, data 
preparation, model building, and model evaluation. However, the study 
did not consider the sixth stage of deployment. The study was divided 
into three major parts (1) identifying the relevant social, demographic, 
and health-related factors associated with the cause of anaemia in 
children aged 6–59 months, (2) building a hybrid machine learning 
model that predicts anaemia among children aged 6–59 months, and (3)
evaluate the performance of the proposed hybrid model. The hybrid 
model was evaluated across different performance metrics, including 
accuracy, precision, sensitivity, specificity, and AUC with 95 % confi­
dence intervals (CIs) through different decision thresholds using You­
den’s J index. Additionally, statistical differences were assessed using 
McNemar’s test to test whether one model outperforms the other, and 
DeLong’s test was used to compare AUCs between ROC curves (Rainio 
et al., 2024).
3.3. Data sources
This study used secondary data from the Tanzania Demographic and 
Health Survey conducted in 2022 (Ministry of Health (MoH)[Tanzania 
Mainland] et al., 2022). This survey measures health issues in Tanzania.
3.4. Data preparation
Data was extracted from a dataset containing 10,783 records. After 
filtering the data to focus on the target age group (6–59 months), only 
4366 records remained. Relevant features were recorded, and categor­
ical values were mapped to facilitate model training. Missing values 
were checked and filled using K-Nearest Neighbours (KNN). Outlier 
values were checked using the Z-score method with a threshold of 
greater than positive or negative three Standard deviations. New fea­
tures were created using the existing ones in the dataset, and those 
features used to create new features were dropped to improve the per­
formance of the model.
3.5. Research tools
This study used the Python programming language (version 3.12.0), 
a popular choice in machine learning research due to its rich machine 
learning libraries. The use of Python, along with important libraries, 
includes Pandas for data cleaning, Matplotlib for data visualization, 
Seaborn for advanced statistical visualization, and Scikit-learn for 
building machine learning models (Sundaram et al., 2023).
4. Factors associated with the cause of anaemia in children aged 
6–59 months
Since the dataset used contained both the numerical and categorical 
features; two different statistical tests were used: One way Analysis of 
Variance (ANOVA) was applied to numerical features such as the child’s 
age in months (child_age), and mother’s age (maternal_age) to compare 
mean differences between the anaemic and not anaemic groups; and the 
Chi-square test was applied for categorical features such as child’s birth 
size (birth_size), and the child’s stunting status (child_stunting) to 
examine whether the distribution of categories varied significantly 
across the outcome groups.
This study found that 14 features were significant in association with 
anaemia in children aged 6–59 months, while 13 features were not. 
These features are present in Table 1 when the value of “TRUE” in col­
umn Significant (p <= 0.05) presents the statistically significant fea­
tures, and the “FALSE” value presents not statistically significant 
features at 95 % Confidence Interval (CI) and p-value<=0.05.
Also, the features associated with anaemia in children aged 6–59 
months that were directly linked to the child were the child’s age in 
months (child_age), the child’s diarrhea status in the last two weeks 
(haddiarr_last2w), the child’s birth size (birth_size), the child’s stunting 
status (child_stunting), the duration of the child’s breastfeeding 
(breastfeed_duration), the child’s fever Status in previous weeks 
(fever_last2w), child’s sex (child_sex), and drugs for intestinal parasites 
status in previous 6 months (hadintestinal_drugs).
In addition, factors associated with anaemia in children, such as 
births in the last 3 years (birth_3yrs), the anaemia level of a mother 
(anemialev_mother), the mother’s age (maternal_age), and whether a 
mother took iron supplements during pregnancy (irontab_preg), were 
associated with the mother’s characteristics. The region (region) and 
source of drinking water (source_drinkwater) were related to anaemia in 
children aged 6–59 months, which was characterized by household 
factors.
5. Building a hybrid machine learning model
5.1. Feature selection
This study used random forest feature selection techniques to find 
the subset of features that improves the performance of the model. The 
random Forest model performed the feature selection technique through 
the repeated evaluation of random subsets of features at each tree split, 
with feature importance calculated by the average reduction in node

5.2. Hyperparameter tuning
The hyperparameters were carefully selected and tuned to ensure the 
robustness and generalizability of all models, RF, ANN, and Hybrid 
Stacking model (RF+ANN with XGBoost as the meta-learner). In this 
study, hyperparameter tuning was performed using a manual and 
literature-guided approach. The hyperparameter values for each model 
were selected based on the evidence from the prior studies and empirical 
testing to ensure both model interpretability and generalization.
5.2.1. Tuning RF model
The ensemble model, Random Forest, was trained using 500 decision 
trees to enhance the generalization and reduce model overfitting. A 
study by Jin et al. (2020) reported that the generalization error de­
creases as the number of trees in the forest increases. The maximum tree 
depth was set to 10. To control overfitting, the minimum number of data 
points needed to split was five split samples or three leaf samples. Even 
more important, the class weights were set to be balanced, automatically 
adjusting the class weights for imbalanced class distributions.

5.2.2. Tuning the ANN model
The second Base model, Artificial Neural Network (ANN), was first 
trained before being evaluated using different evaluation metrics. The 
study used three hidden layers with 128, 64, and 32 neurons to train the 
ANN model. This is known as a pyramidal structure and is widely used in 
deep learning as it allows feature abstraction. A study conducted by 
Goodfellow (2015) highlighted that deeper networks with increased 
depths and decreasing layer sizes are active and successful for abstract 
feature extraction and enhance the generalization, as the first layer 
identifies general patterns and the other layers extract more complex 
information. The ReLU activation was used, which is beneficial for deep 
learning. Additionally, Adam optimizer for adaptive gradient method, 
and finally, early stopping was applied to stop training if no improve­
ment was made to prevent model overfitting. Collectively, these pa­
rameters allowed the ANN to learn meaningful feature representations 
while maintaining generalizability capacity.
5.2.3. Tuning the hybrid model
This hybrid model was formed using two steps: first, getting the 
output probabilities from both RF and ANN, then cross-validation 
stacking was performed to avoid model overfitting. The data were 
then split into five folds or subgroups of about equal size using a strat­
ified K-fold cross-validation, confirming that each fold contains the same 
proportion of the observations.
The second stage involved a meta learner, XGBoost model, which 
was finally trained to learn how best to combine the RF and ANN pre­
dictions for the Hybrid model with carefully tuned parameters. The 
meta-model was tuned using 500 trees, with 80 % of features per tree to 
maximize generalization, a small learning rate of 0.01 for more precise 
boosting, together with a maximum depth of 4, and 80 % of the data per 
tree to prevent overfitting.
5.3. Model development

ensemble approach that combined the strengths of two base models: 
Random Forest (RF) and Artificial Neural Network (ANN), with a meta-

from the base models. According to Rane et al. (2024), this approach 
benefits from the strengths of ensemble learning and neural networks to 
improve generalisation and predictive accuracy and overcome the lim­

learners are trained on the original dataset, and second, a meta-learner is 
trained on the outputs of the base models (predicted probabilities). 
Additionally, the RF model, due to its ensemble decision trees, can 
handle noise well and resist the overfitting problem, and the ANN model 
is capable of dealing with nonlinear relationships and patterns between 
the input and the target features due to its deep learning manner 
(Geethanjali & Valarmathi, 2024). Also, the meta-learner, XGBoost 
model, was chosen for its speed and performance, especially in predic­
tive modelling competitions, and it’s effective in handling several types

this study, at the threshold of 0.4, the hybrid model showed a more 
favourable balance between sensitivity and specificity (86.1 % and 88.0 
% respectively), which is necessary in medical screening like anaemia 
detection, where both false positives and false negatives have a signifi­
cant cost in the life of the children aged 6–59 months.
From a clinical standpoint, false negatives represent the number of 
true anaemic cases classified as not anaemic, leading to delayed treat­
ment, disease development, and avoidable health problems. This type of 
error carries the highest clinical cost. False positives represent the 
number of not-anaemic children being classified as anaemic cases, 
leading to unnecessary treatment and additional resource use, but are 
generally less harmful to the patient.
The trade-off between 0.35 and 0.4 of the Hybrid model carries 
practical implications. At 0.35, the model prioritizes detecting as many 
anaemic cases as possible (fewer FN), but with the cost of treating more 
healthy individuals (higher FP). At 0.4, the model becomes more suit­
able for reducing the number of false alarms related to healthcare re­
sources and patient anxiety. However, there are a few true anaemic 
cases missing.
When compared with ANN, at the Youden-optimized threshold, the 
Hybrid model reduced the number of missed anaemic children (62 vs 79 
FN), which is critical for timely intervention. While the RF model and 
the Hybrid model both performed better, the differences between them 
were not statistically significant. This suggests that the RF model 
appeared to be the best in terms of minimizing missed cases, the Hybrid 
model offered a more balanced trade-off between sensitivity and spec­
ificity, which is clinically safer, reducing missed diagnoses without 
overburdening the system with false alarms.
Overall, the sensitivity (recall) is critical, as missed anaemic cases 
(false negatives) have a greater risk than false positives. The Hybrid 
model achieved a sensitivity of above 86 % at a Youden-optimized 
threshold, with a balanced specificity around 88 %, providing a neces­
sary trade-off between detecting true cases and minimizing false cases. 
The higher specificity of the Hybrid model reduces unnecessary treat­
ment, while maintaining a strong sensitivity, ensuring fewer missed 
diagnoses, indicating the robustness of the Hybrid model to be consid­
ered as a practical decision support tool in screening and early detection 
of anaemia in children, making the Hybrid approach coupled with the 
threshold optimization technique using Youden’s index more suitable 
for real-world anaemia screening programs, especially in situations 
where both under-diagnosis and over-diagnosis carry a significant 
burden.
7. Conclusions and recommendations
7.1. Conclusion
To improve the generalizability and overcome the limitations of 
standalone models especially in dealing with imbalanced cases like 
health datasets, the study concludes to use hybrid machine learning 
model using a stacked ensemble approach combining the strengths of 
two base models, Random Forest (RF) and Artificial Neural Network 
(ANN), with a meta-learner, XGBoost model that aggregated the pre­
dictive probabilities from the base models. The hybrid machine learning 
model and the base models were evaluated for accuracy, precision, 
sensitivity, specificity, and Area Under the Curve (AUC) with 95 % 
confidence intervals (CIs) through different decision thresholds using 
Youden’s J index (0.35, 0.4, 0.45, and 0.5). Additionally, statistical 
differences were assessed using McNemar’s test to test whether one 
model outperforms the other, and DeLong’s test was used to compare 
AUCs between ROC curves. Through threshold optimization, the RF 
model maintained its high sensitivity, but with lower precision and 
specificity while the hybrid model showed a consistently balanced trade- 
off between sensitivity and specificity across all thresholds, and the most 
favourable threshold was 0.4, which provided a more balanced sensi­
tivity and specificity that minimized both false positives and false neg­
atives in anaemia prediction for children aged 6–59 months. Although 
the RF model minimized the false negatives slightly more, the differ­
ences with the Hybrid model were not statistically significant. Clinically, 
the Hybrid model proved safer by ensuring fewer missed cases without 
overburdening the system with false cases. The findings highlight the 
robustness and practical relevance of the Hybrid model as a guiding tool 
for anaemia screening programs. Hence, the study presents a novel 
stacking hybrid model that integrates Random Forest (RF) and Artificial 
Neural Network (ANN) using XGBoost as a meta-learner, joined with 
threshold optimization.
7.2. Contributions
This study has made the following theoretical, methodological, and 
practical contributions.
This study enhances the theoretical understanding of childhood 
anaemia by identifying its multiple causes. It confirms that factors such 
as child-level, maternal characteristics, and household-related factors 
collectively affect the risk of anaemia, thereby recommending multi- 
dimensional health frameworks. Additionally, it also places global 
anaemia classification standards in the context of Tanzania. This con­
tributes to the local implementation of global health guidelines, offering 
insights that bridge international health theory with national 
implementation.
Methodologically, this study presents a novel stacking hybrid model 
that integrates Random Forest (RF) and Artificial Neural Network (ANN) 
using XGBoost as a meta-learner, moving beyond the traditional single- 
model approaches. This model showed strong performance in address­
ing the class imbalance, a challenge in health data analysis. Addition­
ally, integrating the threshold optimization techniques in classification 
tasks to balance sensitivity and specificity, thereby aligning the meth­
odological design with clinical decision-making needs. The study also 
advances the methodological rigor by re-implementing and bench­
marking the state-of-the-art classifiers on the same dataset, and by 
validating the performance improvements through confidence intervals 
and paired statistical significance tests.
In practical terms, this study provides a data-driven framework

7.3. Limitation
The study highly relied on secondary data that contained the social, 
demographic, and health-related factors. Although the dataset is na­
tionally representative, it may lack certain socio-environmental features 
such as micronutrient intake and environmental exposure, which could 
further improve the model’s predictive power and generalization. 
Additionally, the use of features such as breastfeeding duration, child 
birth size, and household water supply, which are not routinely recorded 
in the Health Management Information System (HMIS), limits the direct 
application of the model in real-world clinical settings.
7.4. Recommendations and future work
This study presents the following recommendations and outlines the 
future work. 
i. Health planners and policymakers also improve the campaigns on 
hygiene practices, safe water, and food preparation, and increase the 
deworming and immunization coverage to reduce infectious 
diseases.
ii. Health planners and policymakers also should adopt data-driven 
approaches in designing and evaluating anaemia control programs, 
investing in strengthening health information systems to collect 
high-quality data for predictive modelling routinely.
iii. The study also recommends deploying the hybrid model 
(RF+ANN with XGBoost as a meta-learner) as a decision-support tool 
in community health programs and clinics to identify children at risk 
of anaemia at an early stage.
iv. Based on the relevant social, demographic, and health-related 
factors associated with anaemia in children aged 6–59 months, the 
study recommends that health planners and policymakers design and 
implement the appropriate interventions to eliminate anaemia.
v. The study also recommends further related or longitudinal studies 
incorporating additional socio-environmental variables such as 
household dietary diversity and environmental exposure, to improve 
the model’s generalizability and reliability.
